Output held steady, sick days fell, and meetings turned out to be the thing everyone was happy to lose.
Our year-long experiment with a four-day week
Last January our company of 64 people moved to a four-day, 32-hour week with no change in salary. We agreed to measure the results for twelve months and to go back to five days if the numbers got worse. This is what we found.
What we measured
We tracked the metrics we already reported to the board: revenue per employee, customer support response times, the number of releases shipped, and voluntary turnover. We added a quarterly survey on workload and wellbeing so that we would notice if people were simply compressing five days of stress into four.
Metric Previous year Trial year
Revenue per employee 182,000 187,500
Median support response 3.1 hours 2.8 hours
Releases shipped 41 44
Voluntary turnover 14% 6%
Sick days per person 7.2 4.9
Where the time came from
The honest answer is meetings. Before the trial, the average employee spent eleven hours a week in scheduled meetings. By March that had dropped to six, mostly because recurring status meetings were replaced with written updates that people read when it suited them.
We also introduced two focus blocks per day during which messages were not expected to be answered. Engineers reported the biggest gains, but the finance team was the most enthusiastic, saying month-end close had never felt calmer.
What did not work
Customer-facing roles needed a rota so that someone was always available on Fridays, and for the first two months that rota was unfair to the people with children. We rebuilt it with a simple rule that nobody covers more than one Friday in four.
Some managers struggled to stop measuring presence rather than results. Two of them asked to leave the trial; by the end of the year one of those two had become its most vocal supporter.
The board voted in December to make the four-day week permanent. We will keep publishing the numbers every quarter, because the moment we stop measuring is the moment the old habits come back.
